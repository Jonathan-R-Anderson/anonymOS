## 34. Workstream 7 — Anonymity-Set Size and Effective Anonymity

### 34.1 The finding: guards cut the anonymity set by two to three orders of magnitude

The specification has never stated an anonymity set, and the number is worse than
the reader would assume. Consider an adversary observing traffic at a guard it
controls. The candidate users are not "everyone on the network" — they are the
users who selected that guard. With `U` users each pinning `g` guards drawn from
`R` relays:

```text
   expected users behind one guard  ≈  U · g / R
```

| `U` | `R` | `g` | Users per guard = `A_eff` at the guard | Naive "network size" claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10⁵ | 1 000 | 2 | **200** | 100 000 |
| 10⁵ | 5 000 | 2 | **40** | 100 000 |
| 10⁴ | 1 000 | 2 | **20** | 10 000 |
| 10³ | 200 | 2 | **10** | 1 000 |

**A user is anonymous among hundreds, not among the network.** And under
bandwidth-weighted selection the distribution is far from uniform: users
concentrate on high-capacity relays, so a user who happens to pin a *low*-capacity
guard is in a set of single digits. The worst case, not the average, is what a
targeted user experiences, which is why this workstream reports min-entropy
alongside entropy.

Note what this does to §28. Guards reduce `P_dea` — the probability of *ever*
touching a hostile relay — and simultaneously reduce `A_eff` — the number of
people you look like once one is touched. **They are the same mechanism producing
opposite effects on the two metrics, and no configuration optimises both.** §28.3
and this section are therefore required to report each other's number, and §37
records the joint frontier rather than two independent targets.

### 34.2 The definitions

Anonymity is a property of the adversary's posterior distribution over
candidates, not a count of participants (the Serjantov–Danezis formulation, and
the Díaz *et al.* line of work on entropy-based anonymity metrics):

```text
   after observation O, adversary holds  p(i | O)  over candidate set  C

   H    = − Σ p(i|O) log₂ p(i|O)            average-case uncertainty
   H∞   = − log₂ max_i p(i|O)               worst-case: the most likely suspect
   A_eff = 2^H          (average)      A_eff∞ = 2^H∞      (worst case)
   d    = 1 − H / log₂|C|                   degree of anonymity lost
```

`H∞` is the number a targeted user should be told. A system with `H = 8` bits
(`A_eff = 256`) and `H∞ = 2` bits (`A_eff∞ = 4`) is not a system in which anyone
is anonymous among 256 people; it is one in which the adversary's top suspect
carries probability 0.25.

### 34.3 Anonymity is measured per role, and the roles differ enormously

| Role | Candidate set | What shrinks it | Expected `A_eff` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **User (sender)** | Users sharing the observed guard | Guard pinning, timing, traffic class, isolation context | Hundreds at best (§34.1) |
| **Service (recipient)** | Services reachable at the observation time | Availability pattern, descriptor timing, intro-point set, popularity | Small; a rare service is nearly unique |
| **Relay operator** | Relays with similar capacity and position | Descriptor contents are public by design | Effectively 1 — relays are not anonymous and never claimed to be |
| **Storage provider** | Holders of a shard | Placement diversity constraints deliberately spread holders | Bounded by `r = 8` |
| **Request** | Requests in the same batch or window | Batching for `BULK`; nothing for `INTERACTIVE` | 1 for interactive |
| **Domain** | Names in the namespace | **Registration is public on chain** | Effectively 1 — §18 already says the domain list is public |
| **Session** | Sessions sharing a circuit | Per-destination isolation deliberately separates them | Small by design |
| **Circuit** | Circuits at a relay in the same interval | Multiplexing, padding | Depends entirely on §32 |

Three of these are effectively 1 and always were. **The document's job is to say
so** rather than let a reader assume a uniform anonymity property across roles —
`d` for a relay operator is 0, and that is a design choice, not a failure.

### 34.4 The seventeen fields

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Threat model** | Parameterised by adversary class from §4; the metric is always stated relative to a specific observation by a specific class, since an unconditioned anonymity number is meaningless |
| **Current AXON weakness** | No model, no metric, no measurement. Every anonymity statement elsewhere in the specification is therefore qualitative, including several that read as though they were quantitative |
| **Security objective** | A measurement capability, not a threshold: `H`, `H∞`, `A_eff` computed per role per adversary class, on demand, from a network configuration. Thresholds are set per deployment once the instrument exists |
| **Candidate mechanisms** (for raising it) | Guard sharing — fewer, larger guard sets shared by more users; popularity equalisation for services; batching and delayed forwarding for `BULK`; service replication behind one `ServiceIdentity`; multi-intro-point services; circuit reuse within an isolation context; the §32.3 dual-use cover |
| **Mechanisms rejected** | **Counting nodes** — the error this workstream exists to correct. **Reporting `H` without `H∞`** — hides the targeted case. **Uniform-prior models** — bandwidth weighting makes the prior sharply non-uniform, and assuming uniformity flatters every result |
| **Protocol changes** | None required for measurement. Guard-sharing and popularity-equalisation mechanisms would change selection policy if adopted |
| **Cryptographic changes** | None |
| **DHT changes** | None for measurement. Descriptor-fetch timing is an input to the service-side model and may motivate fetch padding |
| **Economic changes** | None directly. §33's weighting determines the selection prior, hence the anonymity distribution, so the two must be evaluated together |
| **Implementation changes** | An offline analysis tool consuming a network configuration and an adversary specification, emitting the metric set. **Not** a runtime component: computing live anonymity metrics on a node would require exactly the global view the design refuses, and would itself be a leak |
| **Simulation methodology** | Analytic where tractable (guard-sharing distributions), Monte-Carlo otherwise; realistic bandwidth distributions drawn from measured relay populations where available, and flagged as synthetic where not |
| **Adversarial test methodology** | Compute the posterior an adversary can actually form given each observation type, rather than assuming it is uniform over the candidate set — including the compound observation of guard position plus timing plus traffic class |
| **Metrics** | `H`, `H∞`, `A_eff`, `A_eff∞`, `d`, per role, per adversary class, per network size |
| **Milestones** | M7.1 formal model and definitions, reviewed. M7.2 the tool. M7.3 baseline measurement of the current design at `U ∈ {10³, 10⁴, 10⁵}`. M7.4 guard-topology frontier jointly with §28. M7.5 mechanism evaluation. M7.6 publication of the per-role table with real numbers |
| **Exit criteria** | E7.1 The tool produces `H` and `H∞` for all eight roles at three network sizes. E7.2 The §28 guard topologies are re-reported with their `A_eff` cost, and the joint frontier is published. E7.3 The roles whose `A_eff = 1` are named explicitly in §18. E7.4 No anonymity claim anywhere in the AXON specification survives without a scope and a number attached — audited by a documentation pass that is itself an exit criterion |
| **Residual risks** | The model is only as good as its prior. Real user behaviour is more distinguishable than any model assumes. A small network has small `A_eff` under every mechanism, and the early network's honest value is close to 1 |
| **New attacks introduced** | **Guard sharing concentrates**: larger shared guard sets raise `A_eff` and simultaneously raise the value of compromising one guard — the §34.1 trade in the other direction. **Popularity equalisation** for services costs bandwidth and may itself be a distinguisher. **The measurement tool is an attacker's tool**: it computes exactly what an adversary wants to know about a target population |

### 34.5 What this workstream does NOT establish

- Any actual anonymity. It builds the instrument that says how much there is.
- That the numbers will be acceptable. §34.1 suggests they will not be, at any
  plausible early network size.
- That the model captures real behaviour. Timing, usage patterns and application
  fingerprints dominate in practice and are largely outside the model.
- A single headline figure. There is no such thing as "AXON's anonymity set", and
  any future document that quotes one is misusing this work.

## 35. Workstream 8 — Bandwidth Measurement Without a Central Authority

### 35.1 The attack that defeats the current design

R14 specifies "bounded self-report plus delivery receipts plus bond". Delivery
receipts signed by adjacent relays are forgeable by **reciprocal inflation**:

```text
   A and B collude. A signs receipts attesting B delivered 1 Gbps.
   B signs receipts attesting A delivered 1 Gbps.
   Marginal cost: two signatures. No traffic is carried.
   Both gain selection weight proportional to a capacity neither has.
```

Any receipt scheme in which the attester benefits from the attestation, or is
indifferent to it, fails to this. The fix is structural, not cryptographic:
**attestation must come from a party with an incentive to report accurately.**

In AXON that party is the **client**. A client wants working circuits; it gains
nothing from inflating a relay's reported capacity and is actively harmed by
selecting a relay that cannot carry its traffic. Clients are also anonymous,
which is the complication: a client-signed receipt naming a circuit is a
deanonymisation vector, so receipts must be **blind-signed and aggregated**,
reusing the unlinkable-token machinery §14 already specifies for relay payment
(R11). The two mechanisms want the same primitive, which is a genuine
architectural economy.

### 35.2 The composite weight

No single signal is trustworthy; the design combines five, each bounding a
different lie:

```text
   claimed        self-reported capacity          bounds the OPTIMISTIC case
   observed       client-attested delivery        the only incentive-aligned signal
   receipts       blind-signed, aggregated        unlinkable, so clients can attest safely
   stake          bond-bounded claim ceiling      caps inflation by capital
   history        exponentially-weighted record   makes short-term lies expensive
   diversity      prefix/ASN caps                 caps concentration regardless of claim

   weight = min( claimed ,
                 ceiling(stake) ,
                 quantile_q(observed over window) ) · reliability(history)
```

The `min` is the point. A relay's weight is capped by the *most pessimistic*
credible signal, so inflating any one signal gains nothing; an adversary must
inflate all of them simultaneously, and `observed` requires either real capacity
or a population of colluding clients large enough to shift a quantile.

### 35.3 The seventeen fields

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Threat model** | Colluding measurers; fabricated capacity; Sybil measurers; selective service (serve measurers well, everyone else badly); reciprocal inflation; bandwidth withholding; strategic throttling; and joint optimisation with §33's capital attack |
| **Current AXON weakness** | Receipts are attested by parties who benefit from inflation. No quantile logic, no reliability history, no measured `γ`. R14 names the gap and stops |
| **Security objective** | `γ ≤ 2` — an adversary cannot obtain more than twice its true capacity in selection weight — under collusion up to 20 % of clients, with `ε_bw` characterised |
| **Candidate mechanisms** | The §35.2 composite; probabilistic audits with unpredictable challenge timing; multi-observer aggregation with outlier rejection; stake-bounded ceilings; reliability decay; end-to-end throughput attestation by clients rather than hop-by-hop by relays |
| **Mechanisms rejected** | **Central bandwidth authority** — the thing being replaced; also a censorship and compromise point, and Tor's own operational history with bandwidth authorities shows it is not a solved problem even when centralised. **Pure self-report** — trivially gamed. **Relay-to-relay receipts as the primary signal** — defeated by §35.1. **Payment volume as a proxy for bandwidth** — an adversary paying itself inflates for free |
| **Protocol changes** | Client attestation messages; blind-signature issuance and redemption; audit challenge/response; descriptor carries claimed capacity and bond reference |
| **Cryptographic changes** | Blind signatures for unlinkable attestation — **shared with §14's token design and specified once, not twice**. Care required: the same token must not be usable to link an attestation to a payment |
| **DHT changes** | Aggregated measurement records as a record class, signed by aggregators and bonded; individual attestations never published |
| **Economic changes** | Bond-bounded claim ceiling; slashing for a relay whose observed delivery is persistently and materially below its claim; aggregator bonding on the §12.6 pattern |
| **Implementation changes** | `accounting/measure.go`, `accounting/attest.go`, aggregator role, `routing/select.go` integration |
| **Simulation methodology** | Adversary sweeps over colluding client fraction, colluding relay fraction, and selective-service strategies, measuring achieved `γ` — again with the adversary **optimising**, not following a fixed script |
| **Adversarial test methodology** | Reciprocal inflation pairs; a relay that serves audit challenges well and real traffic badly (the hardest case, since audits are distinguishable from traffic unless they are indistinguishable by construction); Sybil client populations manufacturing attestations |
| **Metrics** | `γ` under each collusion level; `ε_bw` distribution against ground truth in the test network; selection-weight error; cost per unit of inflation |
| **Milestones** | M8.1 ground-truth harness on the local network. M8.2 composite weight implemented. M8.3 blind attestation, jointly with §14. M8.4 collusion sweep. M8.5 comparison against a simulated central authority. M8.6 recommended parameters with `γ` stated |
| **Exit criteria** | E8.1 `ε_bw` measured against ground truth on a network where true capacity is known. E8.2 `γ ≤ 2` at 20 % colluding clients — **falsified by the sweep, and failure is a legitimate reported outcome**. E8.3 Reciprocal inflation between colluding relays yields no weight gain — falsified by any gain. E8.4 A selective-service relay is detected within a stated window. E8.5 **The comparison against a central authority is published even if the central baseline wins** — §27.2 forbids suppressing that result |
| **Residual risks** | Majority collusion defeats any peer-measurement scheme. Selective service is hard to detect when audits are distinguishable from real traffic. Clients attesting over circuits add latency and a new message class to fingerprint |
| **New attacks introduced** | **Attestation withholding** — clients that decline to attest starve honest relays of the signal, a slow denial attack. **Aggregator capture** — the aggregator sees the attestation stream; bonding makes it untrusted but not blind. **Blind-token linkage** — if §14's payment tokens and §35's attestation tokens share an issuance epoch, redemption patterns may correlate a payer with an attester, which would undo R11's unlinkability; this must be explicitly prevented and tested |

### 35.4 The comparison the prompt demands, stated honestly

| | Central authority (Tor-style) | AXON composite |
|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Higher — a dedicated measurer with no stake in the outcome | Lower; bounded by quantile logic |
| Manipulation resistance | Good against relays, none against the authority itself | Bounded `γ` against relays; no authority to compromise |
| Failure mode | Authority compromise is total and silent | Degradation is gradual and partially detectable |
| Censorship | The authority can exclude any relay | No party can |
| Operational cost | A handful of well-run scanners | Every client contributes a little |
| Track record | Deployed for years, with known measurement problems | None |

**The honest expectation is that the central baseline measures more accurately.**
The decentralised design is not chosen because it measures better; it is chosen
because R14 refuses the authority, and the workstream's job is to quantify what
that refusal costs in `ε_bw` and `γ`. If the cost turns out to be extreme — if
`γ` cannot be held below 2 at any plausible parameters — that is a finding that
should propagate back to R14 rather than be absorbed quietly.

### 35.5 What this workstream does NOT establish

- Accuracy under majority collusion. Nothing achieves that.
- That the early network can measure anything useful. With few clients, quantiles
  over attestations are noise.
- That audits are indistinguishable from real traffic. If they are
  distinguishable, selective service defeats them, and making them
  indistinguishable is `[NEEDS RESEARCH]`.
- That the blind-token sharing with §14 is safe. It is an economy and a risk, and
  E8.5's companion test exists because sharing a primitive across two subsystems
  is exactly how unlinkability properties are lost.
